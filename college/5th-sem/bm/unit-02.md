# 1. EVOLUTION OF PRINCIPLES OF MANAGEMENT

## 1.1 Meaning

The **principles of management** are general guidelines that help managers make decisions and manage organizational activities effectively.

Management principles did not develop in a single day. They evolved gradually as organizations became larger and more complex.

The evolution of management thought can broadly be studied through:

```text
Early Management Ideas
        ↓
Scientific Management
        ↓
Administrative Management
        ↓
Bureaucratic Management
        ↓
Human Relations Approach
        ↓
Behavioral Approach
        ↓
Quantitative Approach
        ↓
Systems Approach
        ↓
Contingency Approach
        ↓
Modern Management
```

---

# 1.2 Early Management Thought

Management existed even before management became a formal subject.

Large historical projects required:

- Planning
    
- Division of work
    
- Coordination
    
- Leadership
    
- Control
    

### Examples

Construction of:

- Egyptian pyramids
    
- Large irrigation systems
    
- Ancient cities
    
- Military organizations
    

required thousands of people to work together.

However, management principles were not systematically studied during this period.

---

# 1.3 Scientific Management — F.W. Taylor

### Father of Scientific Management

**Frederick Winslow Taylor (F.W. Taylor)** is known as the:

> **Father of Scientific Management**

Scientific management developed mainly during the **late 19th and early 20th century**.

Taylor believed that work should be studied scientifically instead of relying on:

- Guesswork
    
- Traditional methods
    
- Personal judgment

---

## Main Idea

Taylor wanted to find:

> **The one best way of doing a job.**

He studied workers, tools, time and methods to improve productivity.

---

## Principles of Scientific Management

Taylor proposed four major principles.

### 1. Science, Not Rule of Thumb

Traditional organizations often relied on personal experience and trial-and-error methods.

Taylor suggested using:

- Scientific study
    
- Observation
    
- Measurement
    
- Analysis
    

to find the best method.

### Example

Instead of allowing workers to use any method to complete a task, management studies different methods and determines the most efficient one.

---

### 2. Harmony, Not Discord

Management and workers should cooperate instead of fighting with each other.

The objective should be:

> **Common prosperity of both workers and management.**

---

### 3. Cooperation, Not Individualism

Workers and management should cooperate to achieve organizational objectives.

Management should:

- Provide proper tools
    
- Train workers
    
- Establish standards
    
- Provide incentives
    

Workers should follow scientifically developed methods.

---

### 4. Development of Each Person to Greatest Efficiency

Employees should be:

- Properly selected
    
- Properly trained
    
- Properly placed
    

The objective is to develop employees so they can perform their jobs efficiently.

---

# 1.4 Techniques of Scientific Management

Taylor also developed several techniques.

### A. Time Study

Determines the standard time required to complete a task.

### B. Motion Study

Studies workers' movements to eliminate unnecessary movements.

### C. Method Study

Determines the best method of performing a particular job.

### D. Fatigue Study

Studies the amount of rest required to reduce worker fatigue.

### E. Differential Piece Rate System

Workers producing more efficiently could receive higher wages.

The basic idea:

> **Better performance → Higher reward**

---

# 1.5 Administrative Management — Henri Fayol

Another major stage in management evolution was **Administrative Management**.

### Father of Administrative Management

> **Henri Fayol**

Taylor focused primarily on **shop-floor/work efficiency**.

Fayol focused on **management of the entire organization**.

---

## Fayol's Major Contribution

Fayol developed:

- Functions of management
    
- 14 principles of management
    

His principles are:

1. Division of Work
    
2. Authority and Responsibility
    
3. Discipline
    
4. Unity of Command
    
5. Unity of Direction
    
6. Subordination of Individual Interest
    
7. Remuneration
    
8. Centralization
    
9. Scalar Chain
    
10. Order
    
11. Equity
    
12. Stability of Personnel
    
13. Initiative
    
14. Esprit de Corps
    

We will study these in detail later.

# 1.6 Bureaucratic Management — Max Weber

### Main contributor:

> **Max Weber**

Weber developed the concept of **bureaucratic management**.

He believed organizations should operate through:

- Clearly defined rules
    
- Formal authority
    
- Hierarchy
    
- Division of work
    
- Impersonal relationships
    
- Merit-based selection

---

## Features of Bureaucratic Management

### 1. Division of Work

Work is divided into specialized jobs.

### 2. Hierarchy

Authority flows through clearly defined levels.

```text
Top
 ↓
Middle
 ↓
Lower
```

### 3. Formal Rules

Employees follow established rules and procedures.

### 4. Impersonal Relationships

Decisions should be based on rules rather than personal relationships.

### 5. Merit-Based Selection

Employees should be selected based on:

- Qualification
    
- Skills
    
- Competence
    

rather than personal connections.

---

# 1.7 Human Relations Approach

Scientific management focused heavily on:

> **Productivity and efficiency**

But researchers discovered that employees are not simply machines.

Employees also have:

- Social needs
    
- Emotional needs
    
- Psychological needs
    
- Need for recognition
    
- Need for belonging
    

This led to the **Human Relations Approach**.

---

# Elton Mayo and Hawthorne Studies

### Important name:

> **Elton Mayo**

The Hawthorne Studies were conducted at the **Western Electric Company's Hawthorne Works**.

The studies showed that social and psychological factors could influence employee behavior and productivity.

---

## Major Ideas

Employee performance can be influenced by:

- Group relationships
    
- Recognition
    
- Communication
    
- Leadership
    
- Employee morale
    
- Social interaction
    

Therefore:

> **People are an important part of management.**

---

# 1.8 Behavioral Approach

The behavioral approach developed further from the human relations school.

It studies:

> **Human behavior in organizations.**

Important areas include:

- Motivation
    
- Leadership
    
- Communication
    
- Group behavior
    
- Employee satisfaction
    
- Decision-making
    

Important contributors include:

- Abraham Maslow
    
- Douglas McGregor
    
- Frederick Herzberg
    

---

# 1.9 Quantitative Approach

The quantitative approach applies:

- Mathematics
    
- Statistics
    
- Models
    
- Operations research
    

to management decision-making.

### Example

A company can use mathematical models to determine:

- Inventory levels
    
- Production quantities
    
- Transportation routes
    
- Resource allocation
    

This approach helps managers make more objective decisions.

---

# 1.10 Systems Approach

The systems approach views an organization as a **system made up of interconnected parts**.

For example:

```text
Inputs
  ↓
Organization
  ↓
Transformation Process
  ↓
Outputs
  ↓
Feedback
```

### Inputs

- Employees
    
- Money
    
- Materials
    
- Technology
    
- Information
    

### Transformation

Business activities convert inputs into useful products/services.

### Outputs

- Products
    
- Services
    
- Profit
    
- Customer satisfaction
    

### Feedback

Information about performance is used to improve future operations.

---

# 1.11 Contingency Approach

The contingency approach states:

> **There is no single best way to manage every organization or situation.**

The best management method depends on the situation.

Factors may include:

- Organization size
    
- Technology
    
- Employees
    
- Environment
    
- Type of work
    
- Market conditions
    

### Example

A manager may use a strict leadership style during an emergency but a participative style during normal operations.

---

# 1.12 Modern Management Thought

Modern management combines ideas from various approaches.

Modern managers consider:

- Technology
    
- Globalization
    
- Innovation
    
- Competition
    
- Human resources
    
- Data
    
- Sustainability
    
- Customer needs
    
- Organizational culture
    

---

# ⭐ Evolution — Exam Answer

Then explain:

2. **Scientific Management — F.W. Taylor** — emphasized scientific methods and worker efficiency.
    
3. **Administrative Management — Henri Fayol** — focused on overall management and developed 14 principles.
    
4. **Bureaucratic Management — Max Weber** — emphasized hierarchy, rules and formal authority.
    
5. **Human Relations — Elton Mayo** — emphasized social and psychological needs.
    
6. **Behavioral Approach** — focused on human behavior, motivation and leadership.
    
7. **Quantitative Approach** — used mathematics and statistics for decision-making.
    
8. **Systems Approach** — viewed the organization as an interconnected system.

10. **Modern Approach** — combines various approaches to deal with complex business environments.
    

### Easy timeline to memorize:

> **Taylor → Fayol → Weber → Mayo → Behavioral → Quantitative → Systems → Contingency → Modern**

It coordinates human and physical resources to achieve organizational objectives.

---

Management helps organizations establish and achieve goals.

- Human resources
    
- Money
    
- Materials

- Waste
    
- Delays

- Unnecessary costs

- Customer preferences
    
- Economic conditions
    

Management helps organizations adapt.

## 6. Encourages Innovation

- New ideas

- Better processes
    

---

## 7. Employee Development

Management helps employees through:

- Training

## 8. Growth and Expansion

Effective management enables organizations to:

- Increase production
    
- Enter new markets

## 9. Creates Employment

# 3. PROCESS OF MANAGEMENT

Management is a continuous process.

The major functions are:

> **Planning → Organizing → Staffing → Directing → Controlling**

---

# A. PLANNING

## Meaning

Planning means:

> **Deciding in advance what is to be done, how it is to be done, when it is to be done and who will do it.**

---

## Importance of Planning

### 1. Provides Direction

Employees know what they need to achieve.

### 2. Reduces Uncertainty

Planning prepares organizations for future conditions.

### 3. Reduces Waste

Resources can be allocated properly.

### 4. Facilitates Decision-Making

Managers can compare alternatives before choosing one.

### 5. Establishes Standards

Plans provide standards against which actual performance can be measured.

---

# B. ORGANIZING

## Meaning

Organizing means arranging:

- Work
    
- People
    
- Resources
    
- Authority
    
- Responsibilities
    

to achieve organizational goals.

---

## Steps

1. Identify activities
    
2. Divide activities
    
3. Group similar activities
    
4. Assign duties
    
5. Establish authority relationships
    

---

# C. STAFFING

## Meaning

Staffing means:

> **Acquiring, developing and maintaining the right people for the right jobs.**

### Activities

- Manpower planning
    
- Recruitment
    
- Selection
    
- Placement
    
- Training
    
- Development
    
- Promotion
    
- Compensation
    
- Performance appraisal
    

---

# D. DIRECTING

Directing means guiding and motivating employees to achieve objectives.

### Elements

#### 1. Leadership

Ability to influence people.

#### 2. Motivation

Encouraging employees to perform better.

#### 3. Communication

Exchange of information between people.

#### 4. Supervision

Monitoring employees' activities.

---

# E. CONTROLLING

## Meaning

Controlling means ensuring that actual performance matches planned performance.

### Steps

```text
Set Standards
      ↓
Measure Actual Performance
      ↓
Compare
      ↓
Find Deviations
      ↓
Corrective Action
```

### Example

Target = 1,000 units

Actual production = 850 units

Deviation = 150 units

Manager investigates the reason and takes corrective action.

# 7. MANAGEMENT AND ADMINISTRATION

## Administration

Administration is traditionally concerned with:

> **Determining objectives, policies and broad plans of an organization.**

## Management

Management is concerned with:

> **Implementing policies and achieving organizational objectives through planning, organizing, staffing, directing and controlling.**

---

# Difference Between Management and Administration

|Basis|Administration|Management|
|---|---|---|
|Main concern|Policy/objectives|Execution|
|Focus|What should be done?|How should it be done?|
|Nature|Policy-oriented|Action-oriented|
|Level|Traditionally top level|Traditionally middle/lower|
|Role|Formulates policies|Implements policies|
|Scope|Broad|More operational|

### Easy memory:

> **Administration = WHAT**  
> **Management = HOW**

### Important modern view

The distinction is not absolute.

Modern management literature often uses the terms **management and administration interchangeably**, depending on context.

# 9. HENRI FAYOL'S 14 PRINCIPLES OF MANAGEMENT

🔥 **THIS IS ONE OF THE MOST IMPORTANT TOPICS IN YOUR ENTIRE UNIT.**

Henri Fayol developed **14 general principles** to guide managers.

---

# 1. Division of Work

Work should be divided according to specialization.

### Purpose:

- Improve efficiency
    
- Increase productivity
    
- Develop expertise
    

### Example

In a software company:

- Frontend developer
    
- Backend developer
    
- UI designer
    
- Tester
    

each specializes in a particular area.

---

# 2. Authority and Responsibility

### Authority

The right to give orders and make decisions.

### Responsibility

The obligation to perform assigned duties.

Both should go together.

> **Authority without responsibility can lead to misuse, while responsibility without authority makes effective performance difficult.**

---

# 3. Discipline

Discipline means respect for:

- Rules
    
- Agreements
    
- Policies
    
- Organizational standards
    

Good discipline requires:

- Fair rules
    
- Good leadership
    
- Proper supervision
    
- Appropriate penalties
    

---

# 4. Unity of Command

An employee should receive orders from **only one superior**.

### Example

A developer should ideally have one reporting manager who gives official instructions.

### Memory:

> **One Employee → One Boss**

---

# 5. Unity of Direction

Activities having the same objective should have:

> **One plan and one head.**

### Example

All activities related to a particular advertising campaign should follow one common plan.

---

# ⭐ Unity of Command vs Unity of Direction

|Unity of Command|Unity of Direction|
|---|---|
|One employee → One superior|One objective → One plan|
|Deals with authority|Deals with activities|
|Prevents conflicting orders|Ensures coordinated effort|

🔥 Remember this distinction.

---

# 6. Subordination of Individual Interest to General Interest

Organizational interests should be given priority over individual interests.

### Example

An employee should not take a decision that benefits himself but harms the organization.

---

# 7. Remuneration

Employees should receive fair and reasonable compensation.

Good remuneration should:

- Reward employees
    
- Motivate employees
    
- Be fair
    
- Be affordable for the organization
    

---

# 8. Centralization and Decentralization

### Centralization

Decision-making authority is concentrated at higher levels.

### Decentralization

Authority is distributed to lower levels.

Fayol did not suggest that one extreme is always best.

The appropriate degree depends on the organization.

---

# 9. Scalar Chain

Scalar chain refers to the formal line of authority from the highest level to the lowest level.

Example:

```text
CEO
 ↓
General Manager
 ↓
Department Manager
 ↓
Supervisor
 ↓
Worker
```

---

## Gang Plank

In exceptional circumstances, employees at the same level may communicate directly when necessary, rather than strictly following the complete chain, provided proper authority permits it.

### Purpose:

- Faster communication
    
- Faster decisions
    
- Avoid unnecessary delay
    

---

# 10. Order

There should be proper arrangement of:

### Material Order

> **Everything in its proper place.**

### Social Order

> **Right person in the right job.**

---

# 11. Equity

Managers should treat employees with:

- Fairness
    
- Justice
    
- Kindness
    

Employees should not be unfairly discriminated against.

---

# 12. Stability of Personnel

Employees should have reasonable job stability.

High employee turnover can cause:

- Increased recruitment costs
    
- Loss of experience
    
- Lower efficiency
    
- Disruption
    

Therefore, organizations should retain capable employees.

---

# 13. Initiative

Employees should be encouraged to:

- Suggest ideas
    
- Take reasonable initiative
    
- Solve problems
    
- Participate in improvements
    

This promotes:

- Innovation
    
- Motivation
    
- Employee involvement
    

---

# 14. Esprit de Corps

**Esprit de Corps** means:

> **Team spirit and unity among employees.**

Managers should promote:

- Teamwork
    
- Cooperation
    
- Harmony
    
- Unity
    

### Memory:

> **Esprit de Corps = Team Spirit**

# 🎯 HOW TO SCORE BETTER IN THE EXAM

For a **10-mark question**, don't write one huge paragraph.

Use:

### Introduction

2–4 lines defining the concept.

### Main Answer

Use numbered headings.

**1. First point**

Explanation + example if possible.

**2. Second point**

Explanation + example.

Continue.

### Conclusion

2–3 lines summarizing the answer.

For example, for Fayol:

```text
Introduction
       ↓
Meaning of Fayol's principles
       ↓
1. Division of Work
2. Authority & Responsibility
3. Discipline
...
4. Esprit de Corps
       ↓
Conclusion
```

**Underline important keywords** such as _Planning, Coordination, Unity of Command, Authority, Responsibility, Social Responsibility,_ etc. It makes the answer much easier for the examiner to evaluate.

### One important correction from your syllabus

Your syllabus says **“Henry Feyol”**, but the standard spelling is **Henri Fayol**. In the exam, write **Henri Fayol**.

And for **POSDCORB**, remember that the standard expansion is **Planning, Organizing, Staffing, Directing, Coordinating, Reporting, Budgeting**.
