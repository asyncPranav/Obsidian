# UNIT 1 — INTRODUCTION TO BUSINESS MANAGEMENT

## 1. Meaning of Business Management

### What is Management?

Management is the process of **planning, organizing, staffing, directing and controlling** the resources of an organization to achieve its objectives **efficiently and effectively**.

In simple words:

> **Management means getting work done through and with people to achieve organizational goals.**

For example, suppose you start a software company.

You need to:

- Decide what product to build → **Planning**
    
- Divide work among developers → **Organizing**
    
- Hire employees → **Staffing**
    
- Guide and motivate employees → **Directing**
    
- Check whether work is going according to plan → **Controlling**
    

All of these together are **management**.

---

## 2. Definition of Management

### Harold Koontz

> **“Management is the art of getting things done through and with people in formally organized groups.”**

### Mary Parker Follett

> **“Management is the art of getting things done through people.”**

### George R. Terry

> **“Management is a distinct process consisting of planning, organizing, actuating and controlling, performed to determine and accomplish objectives by the use of people and resources.”**

### Easy exam definition

You can write:

> **Management is the process of planning, organizing, staffing, directing and controlling organizational resources in order to achieve predetermined objectives effectively and efficiently.**

This is a very safe definition to remember.

---

# 3. Features / Characteristics of Management

Management has the following important features:

## 1. Goal-Oriented

Management always works toward achieving specific objectives.

For example:

A company's goal may be:

- Increase sales
    
- Increase profit
    
- Improve customer satisfaction
    
- Launch a new product
    

Therefore, management is **goal-oriented**.

---

## 2. Universal Activity

Management is required in almost every organization.

It is needed in:

- Business organizations
    
- Schools
    
- Hospitals
    
- Government departments
    
- NGOs
    
- Banks
    
- Sports organizations
    

Therefore:

> **Management is universal in nature.**

---

## 3. Continuous Process

Management is not a one-time activity.

Managers continuously perform:

**Planning → Organizing → Staffing → Directing → Controlling**

After controlling, new plans may be made.

Therefore, management is a **continuous process**.

---

## 4. Group Activity

Organizations consist of groups of people.

Management coordinates the efforts of different people to achieve a common objective.

For example:

A software company may have:

- Developers
    
- Designers
    
- HR
    
- Marketing
    
- Finance
    

Management coordinates all of them.

---

## 5. Dynamic Process

Business environments continuously change.

Changes may occur in:

- Technology
    
- Customer preferences
    
- Government policies
    
- Competition
    
- Economic conditions
    

Therefore, management must continuously adapt.

---

## 6. Intangible Force

Management cannot be physically seen or touched.

However, its results can be observed.

For example:

- Higher productivity
    
- Better employee performance
    
- Increased profits
    
- Customer satisfaction
    

So management itself is **intangible**, but its effects are visible.

---

## 7. Management is Both Art and Science

Management uses systematic knowledge and principles like a science, but managers must also use personal skills, experience and creativity like an art.

Therefore:

> **Management is both an art and a science.**

---

## 8. Management is a Social Process

Management involves people.

Managers have to deal with:

- Employees
    
- Customers
    
- Suppliers
    
- Government
    
- Society
    

Therefore, management is a **social process**.

---

## 9. Management is Multidisciplinary

Management takes knowledge from different subjects such as:

- Economics
    
- Psychology
    
- Sociology
    
- Mathematics
    
- Statistics
    
- Law
    
- Political science
    

Therefore, management is **multidisciplinary**.

---

## 10. Management is Pervasive

Management is required at:

- Top level
    
- Middle level
    
- Lower level
    

And it is required in different departments.

Therefore, management is **pervasive**.

# 4. Nature of Management

One of the **most important exam questions**:

> **Explain the nature of management.**

The nature of management can be understood by examining whether management is:

1. An Art
    
2. A Science
    
3. A Profession
    

---

# A. Management as an Art

## Meaning of Art

Art means the **practical application of knowledge and skills** to achieve desired results.

Examples:

- Painting
    
- Music
    
- Engineering
    
- Management
    

A manager needs to apply management principles according to the situation.

For example:

Two employees may have different personalities.

A good manager cannot treat both employees exactly the same way.

He needs:

- Experience
    
- Creativity
    
- Communication skills
    
- Leadership skills
    
- Decision-making ability
    

Therefore, management is considered an **art**.

---

## Characteristics of Management as an Art

### 1. Practical Knowledge

Management requires the practical application of theoretical knowledge.

Knowing management theory is not enough.

A manager must know **how to apply it**.

---

### 2. Personal Skill

Every manager develops his own management style.

Two managers may use the same principles but get different results because their skills differ.

---

### 3. Creativity

Managers need creativity to solve business problems.

For example:

If sales are falling, a manager needs to find new ways to attract customers.

---

### 4. Practice

Management skills improve with practice and experience.

The more situations a manager handles, the better he becomes.

---

### 5. Result-Oriented

The purpose of management is to achieve desired objectives.

Therefore, management as an art focuses on **results**.

---

## Conclusion

Management is an art because it requires:

> **Knowledge + Skill + Experience + Creativity + Practice**

---

# B. Management as a Science

## Meaning of Science

Science is a systematic body of knowledge based on:

- Principles
    
- Observation
    
- Experimentation
    
- Cause-and-effect relationships
    

Management has many characteristics of science.

---

## Characteristics of Management as a Science

### 1. Systematic Body of Knowledge

Management has systematic knowledge consisting of:

- Principles
    
- Concepts
    
- Theories
    
- Techniques
    

---

### 2. Principles Based on Observation

Management principles have been developed through observation and experience.

For example:

Division of work can improve efficiency.

---

### 3. Cause-and-Effect Relationship

Management principles establish relationships between actions and results.

For example:

**Better training → Better employee performance**

---

### 4. Universal Application

Management principles can generally be applied in different organizations.

However, they may need modification according to the situation.

---

### 5. Predictive Nature

Management knowledge helps managers predict possible outcomes.

For example:

If employees are properly motivated, their performance may improve.

---

## Why Management is NOT a Pure Science

Management is not like physics or chemistry because human beings are involved.

Human behavior can change.

Therefore:

> Management is a **social science**, not an exact science.

---

# C. Management as a Profession

## Meaning of Profession

A profession is an occupation requiring:

- Specialized knowledge
    
- Education and training
    
- Professional standards
    
- Ethical behavior
    
- Service orientation
    

Examples:

- Doctor
    
- Lawyer
    
- Chartered Accountant
    

Management has some characteristics of a profession.

---

## Management as a Profession — Features

### 1. Specialized Knowledge

Management has a systematic body of knowledge.

Management education is provided through:

- MBA
    
- BBA
    
- Management courses
    
- Professional training
    

---

### 2. Formal Education and Training

Managers can acquire management knowledge through educational institutions and training programs.

---

### 3. Professional Associations

Management has professional organizations and associations that promote management knowledge and practices.

---

### 4. Ethical Code

Managers are expected to behave ethically and responsibly.

They should avoid:

- Fraud
    
- Discrimination
    
- Misuse of authority
    
- Unethical business practices
    

---

### 5. Service Motive

A profession generally has a service motive.

Management also has a responsibility toward:

- Employees
    
- Customers
    
- Owners
    
- Government
    
- Society
    

---

## Why Management is NOT a Full Profession

Management is **not considered a completely developed profession** because:

- A compulsory management degree is not required to become a manager.
    
- There is no universally compulsory professional association.
    
- Managers are not legally required to follow a single professional code.
    

### Conclusion

> Management has many characteristics of a profession, but it is **not a full-fledged profession** like medicine or law.

# 1. Luther Gulick's POSDCORB Concept

This is a **very important exam topic**.

Luther Gulick developed the concept **POSDCORB** to explain major functions performed by administrators/managers.

## POSDCORB

Remember:

> **P O S D C O R B**

|Letter|Meaning|
|---|---|
|P|Planning|
|O|Organizing|
|S|Staffing|
|D|Directing|
|C|Coordinating|
|O|Reporting|
|R|Reporting?|
|B|Budgeting|

Careful: The standard expansion is:

> **P – Planning**  
> **O – Organizing**  
> **S – Staffing**  
> **D – Directing**  
> **CO – Coordinating**  
> **R – Reporting**  
> **B – Budgeting**

So write it as:

**POSDCORB = Planning + Organizing + Staffing + Directing + Coordinating + Reporting + Budgeting**

---

## P — Planning

Planning means deciding:

- What to do?
    
- How to do?
    
- When to do?
    
- Who will do it?
    

Example:

A company decides to launch a new mobile application in six months.

---

## O — Organizing

Organizing means arranging:

- People
    
- Resources
    
- Tasks
    
- Authority
    
- Responsibilities
    

Example:

A project manager divides a software project into frontend, backend and testing teams.

---

## S — Staffing

Staffing means:

- Recruitment
    
- Selection
    
- Training
    
- Placement
    
- Promotion
    
- Development
    

of employees.

---

## D — Directing

Directing means guiding and leading employees toward organizational objectives.

It includes:

- Leadership
    
- Motivation
    
- Communication
    
- Supervision
    

---

## CO — Coordinating

Coordination means bringing different activities and departments together.

Example:

Marketing and production departments must coordinate before launching a product.

---

## R — Reporting

Reporting means providing information about organizational activities and performance to appropriate authorities.

Examples:

- Progress reports
    
- Sales reports
    
- Performance reports
    

---

## B — Budgeting

Budgeting involves:

- Preparing budgets
    
- Accounting
    
- Financial control
    
- Monitoring expenditure
    

Example:

A company prepares a ₹10 lakh budget for a marketing campaign and tracks spending.

# 3. Process of Management

The management process refers to the sequence of activities performed by managers to achieve organizational objectives.

Main functions:

> **Planning → Organizing → Staffing → Directing → Controlling**

Some models also include **coordination**.

---

# 1. Planning

Planning means deciding in advance:

> **What should be done, how, when and by whom?**

### Steps:

1. Identify objectives
    
2. Analyze current situation
    
3. Develop alternatives
    
4. Select best alternative
    
5. Prepare plans
    
6. Implement plans
    

### Example:

A company wants to increase sales by 20% in one year.

---

# 2. Organizing

Organizing involves:

- Dividing work
    
- Grouping activities
    
- Assigning responsibilities
    
- Establishing authority relationships
    

### Example:

A company creates departments:

- HR
    
- Finance
    
- Marketing
    
- IT
    

---

# 3. Staffing

Staffing means ensuring that the organization has the **right people in the right jobs**.

Includes:

- Manpower planning
    
- Recruitment
    
- Selection
    
- Training
    
- Development
    
- Promotion
    
- Compensation
    

---

# 4. Directing

Directing means guiding and motivating employees.

Main elements:

### Leadership

Influencing employees to achieve objectives.

### Motivation

Encouraging employees to perform better.

### Communication

Sharing information between managers and employees.

### Supervision

Monitoring employees' work.

---

# 5. Controlling

Controlling means checking whether actual performance is according to planned performance.

### Steps:

**Set standards**

↓

**Measure actual performance**

↓

**Compare actual performance with standards**

↓

**Identify deviations**

↓

**Take corrective action**

### Example:

Target sales = ₹10 lakh

Actual sales = ₹8 lakh

Deviation = ₹2 lakh

Management investigates why sales are lower and takes corrective action.

---

# 7. Management and Administration

This is another **frequently asked difference question**.

## Meaning of Management

Management is concerned mainly with:

> **Planning, organizing, staffing, directing and controlling organizational activities.**

## Meaning of Administration

Administration is generally concerned with:

> **Determining overall policies, objectives and broad direction of an organization.**

---

# Difference Between Management and Administration

|Basis|Management|Administration|
|---|---|---|
|Main role|Implements policies|Determines policies|
|Focus|Execution|Policy formulation|
|Level|Often associated with middle/lower levels|Traditionally associated with top level|
|Nature|Operational|Strategic/policy-oriented|
|Main question|How to achieve objectives?|What objectives/policies should be adopted?|
|Example|Implementing a marketing plan|Deciding the organization's marketing policy|

### Important note

Modern management thinkers often use **management and administration interchangeably**, so the distinction is not absolute.

### Easy memory:

> **Administration decides WHAT.**  
> **Management focuses on HOW.**

# 8. Henry Fayol's 14 Principles of Management

🔥 **VERY IMPORTANT — HIGH CHANCE OF EXAM QUESTION**

Henri Fayol proposed **14 principles of management**.

You should memorize all 14.

---

# 1. Division of Work

Work should be divided among employees according to their specialization.

### Benefit:

Specialization increases:

- Efficiency
    
- Productivity
    
- Skill
    

### Example:

In software development:

- One person handles frontend
    
- One handles backend
    
- One handles testing
    

---

# 2. Authority and Responsibility

Managers must have authority to give orders and responsibility to perform their duties.

> **Authority and responsibility should go together.**

If a manager is responsible for a project but has no authority to make decisions, effective management becomes difficult.

---

# 3. Discipline

Employees should respect:

- Rules
    
- Agreements
    
- Organizational policies
    

Discipline requires:

- Good leadership
    
- Clear rules
    
- Fair penalties
    
- Proper supervision
    

---

# 4. Unity of Command

An employee should receive orders from **only one superior**.

### Example:

A developer should not receive conflicting instructions from two different managers.

### Memory:

> **One employee → One boss**

---

# 5. Unity of Direction

Activities having the same objective should have:

- One plan
    
- One head
    

### Example:

All marketing activities for one campaign should follow a common plan.

### Difference from Unity of Command

**Unity of Command:**

> One employee → One boss

**Unity of Direction:**

> One objective → One plan + One head

🔥 Very important difference.

---

# 6. Subordination of Individual Interest to General Interest

Organizational interests should be placed above individual interests.

Example:

An employee should not make decisions that benefit himself but harm the organization.

---

# 7. Remuneration

Employees should receive fair and reasonable compensation for their work.

Good remuneration should be:

- Fair
    
- Motivating
    
- Reasonable for both employee and organization
    

---

# 8. Centralization and Decentralization

Centralization means decision-making authority is concentrated at higher levels.

Decentralization means authority is distributed to lower levels.

Fayol believed the appropriate balance depends on the organization and situation.

---

# 9. Scalar Chain

Scalar chain means the formal line of authority from top management to lower levels.

Example:

```text
CEO
 ↓
General Manager
 ↓
Department Manager
 ↓
Supervisor
 ↓
Worker
```

### Fayol's concept of Gang Plank

In emergencies, employees at the same level may communicate directly when permitted, rather than strictly following the entire chain.

---

# 10. Order

There should be:

### Material order

Everything should be in its proper place.

### Social order

The right person should be in the right job.

> **Right person + Right place = Order**

---

# 11. Equity

Managers should treat employees with:

- Fairness
    
- Justice
    
- Kindness
    

There should be no unnecessary discrimination.

---

# 12. Stability of Personnel

Employees should have reasonable job stability.

High employee turnover can reduce:

- Efficiency
    
- Experience
    
- Productivity
    

Therefore, organizations should try to retain capable employees.

---

# 13. Initiative

Employees should be encouraged to:

- Think
    
- Suggest ideas
    
- Take reasonable initiative
    
- Develop new solutions
    

This improves motivation and innovation.

---

# 14. Esprit de Corps

French term meaning:

> **Team spirit / unity among employees**

Managers should promote:

- Teamwork
    
- Harmony
    
- Cooperation
    
- Unity
    

### Easy memory:

> **Esprit de Corps = Team Spirit**
